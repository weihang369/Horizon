Grandma's recipe notebook (typed up by Hana, Sunny Hollow)

p. 1. For Hana, Rin and whoever they bring home. Cook for people, not for photographs.

p. 2. Yuzu cake: zest two yuzu into the sugar first and rub it with your fingers until it smells like winter sunshine. Don't skip this. The juice goes in the glaze, never the batter.

p. 3. Yuzu cake, the rest: 200 g butter, 200 g of that yuzu sugar, four eggs, 200 g flour, a pinch of salt. Bake in a slow oven until a skewer comes out clean. Glaze while warm with icing sugar and the juice. It keeps three days, if nobody knows where it is.

p. 4. Miso marinade for fish: two spoons miso, one of mirin, one of sugar. Leave the fish in it overnight. Wipe it off before grilling or it burns.

p. 5. Sushi rice for a crowd: three cups of rice and a splash more vinegar than you think. A spoon of pineapple juice in the vinegar keeps it bright when the fish is rich. Your grandfather never noticed.

p. 6. Fishing-day rice balls: pack them the night before, wrap them in a cloth, and give the biggest one to whoever drives.

p. 7. Tamagoyaki: three eggs, a spoon of sugar, a little dashi. Roll it in thin layers. If it tears, nobody minds; call it rustic.

p. 8. Rainy-day soup: miso, tofu and whatever greens are wilting. If a child refuses it, call it "dragon broth" and serve it in the good bowls.

p. 9. Fever soup: the same soup with ginger and an egg stirred in. Bring it to the bedroom. Don't ask if they want it.

p. 10. Strawberry daifuku for the shop's opening day: wrap each strawberry in sweet bean paste, then in soft mochi. Dust your hands with starch, not flour.

p. 11. Flowers on the table should never be taller than your elbow. People want to see each other when they eat.

p. 12. A good dinner has one dish everyone loves and one dish someone has never tried. Arguments about the second one are part of the meal.

p. 13. Pickled plums: salt, patience and a dark cupboard. Check them in June.

p. 14. Leftover rice becomes tomorrow's onigiri. Salt your hands, not the rice, and press as if you were holding a small bird.
