**Search timing (task 6.3).** On a test database holding 3,000 pieces for one character, every one with a stored
vector, and a worst case where every query word is in every piece (`test_search_timing_m12.py`, 110 searches each):

| Search | p50 | p99 |
|---|---|---|
| keywords only (no query vector) | 30 ms | 46 ms |
| keywords and two dense texts | 56 ms | 69 ms |

The target was 50 ms at p99. What costs, measured step by step:
- each flat KNN over the partition is ≈ 11 ms (two dense texts: 22 ms);
- the 20 stored vectors MMR compares cost ≈ 19 ms;
- FTS costs ≈ 9 ms;
- MMR itself costs ≈ 2 ms, after making it incremental.

Reading a vector back from sqlite-vec is the slow part:
- by key, ≈ 1.5 ms each;
- as an `IN` list, ≈ 20 ms (a partition scan);
- with the KNN query, +19 ms;
- as one statement of key lookups (what ships), ≈ 19 ms for 20.

Dense searches miss the target by about 20 ms. The test guards keyword searches at 50 ms and dense ones at 100 ms
until the user decides.

**Findings while building:**
- **A search that matches nothing is "not found".** Jev call 2 never runs and nothing failed. It was a fallback in the
  first build, which left a debate turn on an unrelated motion with no trace.
- **In 1:1 a new message queues behind the reply; it doesn't supersede it.** M7 built it that way. Stop (`clear()`) is
  1:1's cut for the rewrite and the checks; in group a newer message cancels the deep work (G5). The storms cover both.
- **The near-copies scenario was stronger than MMR at λ 0.7 guarantees.** The retrieval spec said "at most one of the
  top 3 copies among the 8". MMR pushes a copy down only below pieces of relevance ≳ 0.56. The scenario now says what
  MMR does: with seven others of similar relevance, at most one copy is kept. λ is still compared in phase 11.
- **A director's note drops the plan made ahead's deep work at once.** That plan is stale anyway (its fingerprint
  holds the note), and dropping it now records its checks `cancelled` instead of `late`. M10's own plan still runs out
  as M10 left it.
- **Test doubles.** The fake answers every "needs documents?" no unless a test sets it, and a queued streamed reply
  waits for a chat request, so the gate questions riding a plan don't take it. A held embedding gives up its activity
  token, so virtual time can pass the 400 ms wait.

**The user's choice on 6.3 (2026-10-15):** accept and record. Dense searches keep the 100 ms guard, the 69 ms p99 is
recorded against the 50 ms target, and the step is judged inside check 2's end-to-end timing (next to Jev's ≈ 500 ms
`deep_check`).

### 11.1 Check 5: Jev's output against K7 (`check5.jsonl`)

`horizon eval deep-check`, built for this check, asks 20 `deep_check` passages, 5 set checks and the two nouls on 20
messages, each exactly as live asks it. **K7's reading of the output holds:**
- **The levels' probabilities came back on 20 of 20 passages.** So `unit` (Σ k·p_k / 2) is a real weighted score, not
  a chosen level at 1.0.
- **Every passage's top level was the expected one (20/20).** The weighted score separates them widely:

| Expected | Passages | `unit` min / median / max |
|---|---|---|
| answers | 7 | 0.96 / 0.99 / 1.00 |
| partly (the held half of a two-part question) | 6 | 0.47 / 0.50 / 0.50 |
| doesn't help (another question's paragraph, same character) | 7 | 0.00 / 0.00 / 0.14 |

  The keep rule's starting `t_keep` and `t_found` of 0.6 sit cleanly between partly (≤ 0.50) and answers (≥ 0.96).
  11.5 chooses them on dev.
- **The set check separates all five pairs at `t_set` 0.5.**
  - The three complementary pairs read 0.87, 0.89 and 0.65. The least sure is the fibre pair, popcorn and nuts from

  - The two swapped pairs read 0.11 and 0.21.
- **"Standalone?"** put the five follow-ups at 0.13–0.38 and the five standalone messages at 0.47–0.81. The closest are

### 11.2 Check 4: `pack@2` on real documents (local, free; `check4.json`)

**The documents.** The user approved the two downloads on 2026-10-15:
- the CDC's "If you have monkeypox: self care" in Simplified Chinese (stacks.cdc.gov record 123575, marked Public
  Domain);
- the BLS "Employment Situation" release for September 2026 (`bls.gov/news.release/pdf/empsit.pdf`, a US federal
  work).

Both sit in `evals/check4/`. D22 says that folder is gitignored, but it wasn't; this change adds the rule, and neither
file is committed. `docs/ai/checks/m12/check4.py` converts the two PDFs with the app's own Docling converter and chunks
them as paged documents. It also chunks the 15 corpus documents as Markdown (268 pieces).

**The first run crashed: `pack@2` failed on the BLS release** (`IndexError`, then `ValueError` on a smaller case).
- A table whose header row is ≈ 325 tokens repeats that header at the top of each later part.
- When the next row is long, header and row don't fit in one piece, so no row boundary fits.
- The cut then falls back to a sentence end, and "Sept. 2025" inside the header counts as one. So the cut landed
  inside the repeated header, and a piece held only header text, with no source offsets.

**Fixed in both twins:** a repeated header that can't share a piece with its first row is dropped, and that part starts
at the row. A new shared fixture, `table_wide_header.json`, crashes the old code and passes both suites.

| Point | Result |
|---|---|
| headings carried forward | yes: 48 BLS pieces and 3 of the 5 CDC pieces open with their heading; every piece has its heading path |
| tables keep their lines and header rows | yes: 595 BLS table pieces, one row per line; 557 repeat the header row (the rest are first parts, or the dropped wide header above) |
| a mid-sentence page break gives `pp. N–M` | yes: the household survey paragraph across pages 1 and 2 is one piece, `pp. 1–2` |
| Chinese cut at `。` | yes: the CDC pieces end at `。` ("痊愈。", "作用。") |

**Two findings for the user (not changed):**
- **Docling's `<!-- image -->` placeholder lines are kept as text.**
  - On the CDC sheet, one piece is six image markers and nothing else (16 tokens), and another is the masthead "猴 痘"
    plus a marker (8 tokens). Both are indexed and embedded like any piece.
  - Dropping marker lines in the chunker (both twins) would remove them. It changes every PDF's pieces, so existing
    PDFs re-chunk on their next reindex.
- **Converter output, not chunking:**
  - the Chinese PDF's line wraps come through as spaces inside words ("盖 住皮疹");
  - the BLS tables keep their dotted leaders ("Employed. . . . .") and very long `|---|` separators, which cost tokens
    in every table piece.
