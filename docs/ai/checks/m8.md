### Cache hit below X6's 80 %: provider-side, not our prefix

The `long` runs reported a provider cache hit of 31–54 % (English, group) and 94 % (Malay), against X6's ≥ 80 %. The
offline probe ($0, fake provider, six `agent` turns) shows each request is **exactly the previous request plus the new
lines**: nothing earlier changes, and no tail message sits between turns. So the prefix the cache needs is there.
DeepSeek's disk cache is best-effort and is built a few seconds after a request; the eval drives turns back to back,
seconds apart, which likely outruns it (the Malay run, whose turns were slower, hit 94 %). **No change in M8.** M15's
latency suite measures the cache hit at human pacing; if it stays low there, that is the time to look at provider
routing.

### What M8 found

1. **Temperature 1.3 breaks long chats** on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (word salad, 32–47 % cut at `max_tokens`); `reply.chat`
   is 1.0 (D34).
2. **The model ignores format instructions.** The `naive` emotion tag comes back valid 5–8 % of the time; a bare
   `<neutral>` leaked to the user until D37, and 33 % of Meridian replies still open with the emotion as a word.
3. **The reply language follows the persona, not the user,** until a per-turn cue says otherwise (D35: Malay replies
   from 0–3 % to 91 %).
4. **The provider's cache hit is 19–56 % in English** against X6's 80 % (93 % in Malay); our prefix is byte-identical,
   so it's on the provider side. It roughly doubles reply cost over the estimates.
5. **Judges pass B4 but miss subtle failures** (unsupported claims 0/4, pushback 1/3, relationship 0/2 of the real
   ones); `judge_voice` isn't validated. Planted failures are caught.
6. **One-sided long chats push characters to disengage or escalate** (26 % of Meridian replies push the GP or close
   the room); real group pushback failures exist (Takeshi agrees, Victor concedes).
7. **The response store can't replay a long chat**: background work finishes at different moments, so later requests
   differ. Replays are reliable for single calls (drafts, summaries, judges), not multi-turn runs.
